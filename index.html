I am a Senior Applied Scientist at Microsoft Research on the BioML team, where I build generative AI models that design new, functional proteins to advance human health. I care about understanding the fundamental forces that drive biological systems, and using that understanding to rationally design efficient, bioinspired technologies, faster than we ever could before.

I completed my Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering at the University of Washington, where I was an NSF GRFP Fellow. My research focused on developing enhanced sampling methods to probe the interfacial phenomena that govern biology at the microscale.

Generative models for protein design Diffusion and language models that generate new proteins — EvoDiff and the Dayhoff Atlas. Benchmarks and evaluation for protein ML Making protein machine learning measurable and reproducible — FLIP2 and ProtNote. Molecular simulation and biophysics Enhanced sampling to uncover the structure and energetics of biomolecules at interfaces.
